By 5 November Ponko could draw 3D scenes, but nothing in them moved. That
day we added physics, then animated characters, then navigation meshes so
characters can find their own way around.

## Physics

3D physics uses [Rapier](https://rapier.rs/), the same library as our 2D
physics, and is behind the `physics3d` feature. Rigid bodies and colliders
are just components: add them to an entity and its transform follows the
simulation.

- Shapes: spheres, boxes, capsules, convex hulls, triangle meshes and
  heightfields. There's friction, restitution, sensors and collision groups.
- The simulation steps at a fixed 50 Hz. Positions are interpolated between
  steps when drawing, so movement looks smooth at any frame rate.
- Terrain colliders match a terrain tile's layout exactly. Colliders can
  stream in around focus points, so in a big world only the area near the
  player needs to be solid.
- Queries: raycasts, sphere and capsule casts, and overlap tests, all with a
  filter.

## Character controller

`Character3D` is a kinematic capsule you can use for the player or for NPCs.
You give it the velocity you want and it deals with the rest: it has a slope
limit and a step height for stairs and kerbs, sticks to the ground when going
downhill, and pushes light bodies out of the way. You can ask it whether it's
on the ground.

For first-person games, `ctx.set_cursor_locked` captures the mouse and
`ctx.mouse_motion` gives you the raw movement.

## Cars

Vehicles use raycast wheels, the same model as most engines' wheel
colliders. Each physics step, every wheel casts a ray down from where it's
mounted. A spring and damper push the chassis up, the motor and brake torque
you set spin the wheel, and the tyre's friction curves push the chassis
forward and sideways.

You can have up to 8 wheels, so trucks and buggies work as well as cars. If
you tuned a car for this model in another engine, the same numbers should
work here. Every tick you set torque and steering, and you can read back each
wheel's contact, suspension compression, slip, rpm and pose for rendering.

<Callout type="tip">
  Give a vehicle's body a mass and a low center of mass. Without them it rolls
  over in the first sharp turn. The `vehicle` example has a tuned car to start
  from.
</Callout>

## Ragdolls, joints and contacts

- Joints connect bodies to each other.
- Ragdolls hand a skinned character's skeleton over to physics. Every bone
  you pick becomes a capsule, attached to its parent with a joint that limits
  swing and twist. You can apply forces in the same frame (for a hit or an
  explosion), and from then on physics drives the pose.
- Contact modification lets the game change contacts while they're being
  resolved. One-way pushing lets a body push another without getting pushed
  back.

## Animation

Skinned meshes landed the same day: skeletons, glTF skins and GPU skinning.
On top of that we added:

- Animation clips, state machines and layers, so a character can run and wave
  at the same time.
- Inverse kinematics, for planting feet on uneven ground and putting hands on
  handles.
- Morph targets (blend shapes) for faces, which clips can drive too.
- 3D audio, with sounds positioned in the world.

## Navigation

For characters that walk around on their own, you can bake navigation meshes
from the level. Large levels are baked in tiles. Crowd avoidance is optional
and stops agents from walking through each other. When obstacles move,
dynamic carving cuts holes in the navmesh without a full rebake.

To get started, see [3D physics](/guide/physics3d.html) and
[3D navigation meshes](/guide/navigation3d.html) in the guide.

A 3D world that only draws is a diorama. On 5 November Ponko's started to
move: physics, then animated characters, then the meshes they find their way
around on.

## Physics

3D physics is built on [Rapier](https://rapier.rs/), the same library as
Ponko's 2D physics, and lives behind the `physics3d` feature. Bodies are
components: give an entity a rigid body and a collider, and its transform
follows the simulation.

- **Shapes**: spheres, boxes, capsules, convex hulls, triangle meshes and
  heightfields, with friction, restitution, sensors and collision groups.
- **A fixed 50 Hz step.** Drawn positions are interpolated between steps, so
  motion looks smooth at any frame rate.
- **Terrain colliders** in the exact layout of a terrain tile, and collider
  streaming near focus points, so a big world is solid only around the player.
- **Queries**: rays, sphere and capsule casts, and overlap tests, with a
  filter.

## A character that walks

`Character3D` is a kinematic capsule for player characters and NPCs. You
set the velocity you want, and it handles the rest: a slope limit, a step
height for stairs and kerbs, snapping to the ground going downhill, and
pushing light bodies out of the way. It reads back whether it's grounded.

For first-person games, `ctx.set_cursor_locked` grabs the mouse and
`ctx.mouse_motion` reads raw movement.

## Cars

Vehicles use **raycast wheels**, the classic wheel-collider model. Every
physics step, each wheel casts a ray down from its mount, pushes the chassis
up with a spring and a damper, spins from the motor and brake torque you give
it, and pushes the chassis along through its tyre's friction curves.

A vehicle takes up to 8 wheels, so cars, trucks and buggies all work, and a
car tuned for this model in another engine keeps its numbers here. Each tick
you set the torques and steering angle, and read back each wheel's contact,
suspension compression, slip, rpm and pose for drawing.

<Callout type="tip">
  Give a vehicle's body a mass and a low center of mass. Without them it rolls
  over in the first sharp turn. The `vehicle` example has a tuned car to start
  from.
</Callout>

## Ragdolls, joints and contacts

- **Joints** link bodies together.
- **Ragdolls** hand a skinned character's skeleton over to physics. Each
  chosen bone becomes a capsule, held to the one above it by a joint limited
  in swing and twist. You can push the bodies in the same frame, for a hit or
  an explosion, and they pose the skeleton from then on.
- **Contact modification** lets a game change contacts as they happen, and
  **one-way pushing** lets one body shove another without being pushed back.

## Things that animate

The same day brought skinned meshes: skeletons, glTF skins and GPU skinning.
On top of them:

- **Animation clips, state machines and layers**, so a character can run and
  wave at the same time.
- **Inverse kinematics**, for feet on uneven ground and hands on handles.
- **Morph targets** (blend shapes) for faces, driven by clips too.
- **3D audio**, with sounds placed in the world.

## Finding the way

Navigation meshes are baked from the level, for characters that need to walk
somewhere on their own. Large levels bake in tiles. Crowd avoidance, which is
opt-in, keeps agents from walking through each other. Dynamic carving cuts
holes in the mesh where obstacles move, without baking it again.

[3D navigation meshes](/guide/navigation3d.html) in the guide to start.

On the evening of 3 November Ponko drew its first 3D mesh. Three days
later it drew physically based worlds with real-time global illumination. This is what was added, in
roughly the order it arrived.

All of it is behind the `3d` Cargo feature, so a 2D game doesn't carry it.
3D models are components on the same ECS as everything else: a
`Transform3D` and a `Model` on an entity, and the renderer finds them.

## The foundation

- **Meshes and materials**: cubes, planes and spheres, your own triangles, or
  glTF files with their normal, occlusion, metallic-roughness and emissive
  maps. Materials are physically based.
- **HDR lighting** with a depth prepass, so each pixel is lit once, and
  post-processing: exposure, bloom, white balance, ACES, AgX or neutral tone
  mapping, colour lookup tables and vignette.
- **Cascaded sun shadows**: up to four shadow maps, sharp near the camera and
  reaching far, blended where they meet.
- **A physical sky** with Rayleigh and Mie scattering, a sun that reddens as
  it sets, stars at night, and height and distance fog that glows toward the
  sun.

## Your own shaders

Materials can run shaders you write in WGSL. A shader defines `vertex` to
move vertices, `surface` to change each pixel, or both, and the engine still
lights and shadows the result. They hot-reload when you save the file. The
`shaders` example has water, wind and a procedural pattern.

The same goes for whole-frame effects (`fn effect(p: Pixel)`) and compute
passes that run once per texel of a render target, for ripples, trails in snow
or wet ground. Render targets give you mirrors, monitors and planar
reflections.

## Big worlds

- **Terrain** from heightfield tiles, drawn at four levels of detail with
  skirts that hide the cracks, and terrain materials that blend up to 8 ground
  layers. On steep ground they're projected from the sides, so cliffs don't
  stretch.
- **Streaming**: `CellStreamer` says which cells of the world to load and drop
  around a point, nearest first and a few per frame, and `Job` builds tiles
  on worker threads.
- **LOD groups** with dithered crossfades, and **occlusion culling** against
  an earlier frame's depth.

## Light, and a lot of it

Point and spot lights are binned into clusters per camera, and each pixel
lights only its cluster's lights. The packing works on WebGL2 too, with no
storage buffers.

## Image quality

By 6 November the renderer had the techniques modern games rely on:

- **Temporal antialiasing** with per-object motion vectors, plus FXAA and SMAA.
- **Ground-truth ambient occlusion**, with half-resolution and temporal
  modes, and contact shadows in the same pass.
- **Screen-space reflections**, blurred by roughness.
- **Image-based lighting** from the sky, and reflection probes.
- **Real-time global illumination** from probe volumes.
- **Light shafts**, where shadows cut through the fog's glow toward the sun.

## How fast

The `3d` example spins N tinted cubes with PBR spheres, a sun and shadows.
The same scene was built in Unity 6 to compare:

That's on an M4 Max in release builds. The
[benchmarks page](/guide/performance.html) has the workload and how to run
it yourself. We'd like to hear what it gives on your machine.

Measuring is built in too. `ctx.perf.frame_stats()` splits a frame into
systems, rendering and waiting, with GPU time for each pass and every shadow
cascade. There's a performance overlay (`PONKO_HUD=1`), and you can record
Perfetto traces to find hitches.

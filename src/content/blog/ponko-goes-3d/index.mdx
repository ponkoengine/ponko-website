Ponko drew its first 3D mesh on the evening of 3 November. Three days later
it had physically based rendering and real-time global illumination. Below is
everything that went in, roughly in the order we built it.

It's all behind the `3d` Cargo feature, so 2D games don't pay for it. 3D
models use the same ECS as everything else: put a `Transform3D` and a
`Model` on an entity and the renderer picks it up.

## The basics

- Meshes and materials. You can use cubes, planes and spheres, your own
  triangles, or glTF files with normal, occlusion, metallic-roughness and
  emissive maps. Materials are PBR.
- HDR lighting with a depth prepass, so each pixel is only shaded once. Then
  post-processing: exposure, bloom, white balance, ACES, AgX or neutral tone
  mapping, colour LUTs and vignette.
- Cascaded shadows from the sun, with up to four shadow maps. They're sharp
  close to the camera, reach a long way out, and blend at the seams.
- A physical sky with Rayleigh and Mie scattering. The sun goes red as it
  sets, stars come out at night, and height and distance fog glows toward
  the sun.

## Custom shaders

Materials can use your own WGSL shaders. Define `vertex` to move vertices,
`surface` to change pixels, or both, and the engine still handles lighting
and shadows for you. Shaders hot-reload when you save. The `shaders` example
has water, wind and a procedural pattern.

Full-screen effects (`fn effect(p: Pixel)`) work the same way, and so do
compute passes that run once per texel of a render target, for things like
ripples, trails in snow or wet ground. Render targets also give you mirrors,
monitors and planar reflections.

## Large worlds

- Terrain from heightfield tiles, drawn at four levels of detail, with
  skirts to hide the cracks between them. Terrain materials blend up to 8
  ground layers and switch to side projection on steep slopes, so cliffs
  don't look stretched.
- Streaming. `CellStreamer` tells you which cells of the world to load and
  unload around a point, closest first and a few per frame, and `Job` builds
  tiles on worker threads.
- LOD groups with dithered crossfades, and occlusion culling against the
  depth of an earlier frame.

## Lots of lights

Point and spot lights are sorted into clusters for each camera, and each
pixel only looks at the lights in its cluster. This works on WebGL2 too,
without storage buffers.

## Image quality

By 6 November we had most of what you'd expect from a current renderer:

- Temporal antialiasing with per-object motion vectors, plus FXAA and SMAA.
- Ground-truth ambient occlusion (GTAO), with half-resolution and temporal
  modes, and contact shadows in the same pass.
- Screen-space reflections, blurred by roughness.
- Image-based lighting from the sky, and reflection probes.
- Real-time global illumination from probe volumes.
- Light shafts where shadows cut through the fog's glow around the sun.

## Performance

The `3d` example spins N tinted cubes next to some PBR spheres, with a sun
and shadows. We built the same scene in Unity 6 to compare:

Both are release builds on an M4 Max. The workload and how to run it are on
the [benchmarks page](/guide/performance.html), and we'd like to know what
numbers you get on your own machine.

There are profiling tools built in as well. `ctx.perf.frame_stats()` breaks a
frame down into systems, rendering and waiting, with GPU time for every pass
and shadow cascade. `PONKO_HUD=1` turns on a performance overlay, and you can
record Perfetto traces to track down hitches.
